Lil Durk's Federal Trial Is Underway

Prosecutors say revenge over King Von's death led to a murder-for-hire plot targeting Quando Rondo. Lil Durk's defense says the government has the wrong man. Now a federal jury has to decide what the evidence actually proves.

THE BLIND TRUTH • AUGUST 25, 2026 • TRIAL DEVELOPING

The federal trial that could determine the future of Grammy-winning Chicago rapper Lil Durk has officially begun in Los Angeles.

Durk, whose legal name is Durk Devontay Banks, has pleaded not guilty to federal charges connected to an alleged murder-for-hire plot targeting rapper Quando Rondo.

The intended target survived. His cousin, 24-year-old Saviay'a “Lul Pab” Robinson, did not.

IMPORTANT: PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE Lil Durk has not been convicted in this case. The government's accusations are allegations. The prosecution must prove the charges against him beyond a reasonable doubt.

What Happened in Los Angeles?

The case centers on an August 19, 2022 shooting near the Beverly Center in Los Angeles.

Federal prosecutors say members and associates of Durk's Only the Family, or OTF, collective traveled from Chicago to Los Angeles after learning that Quando Rondo — whose legal name is Tyquian Bowman — was in the city.

Prosecutors allege members of the group tracked Bowman before gunfire erupted near a gas station. Bowman survived. His cousin Robinson was struck and killed.

Why Prosecutors Say It Happened

To understand the government's theory, the story goes back nearly two years earlier.

In November 2020, Chicago rapper Dayvon “King Von” Bennett was killed following a confrontation outside an Atlanta nightclub.

King Von was closely associated with Durk. Federal prosecutors now argue that his death provided the motive for what happened in Los Angeles in 2022.

THE PROSECUTION'S CASE

Assistant U.S. Attorney Daniel Weiner told jurors during opening statements that Durk became consumed with revenge following King Von's death.

According to prosecutors, Durk allegedly used his money and influence to facilitate a plot to locate and kill Quando Rondo.

The government alleges Durk communicated using coded language and offered money in connection with targeting Bowman.

Prosecutors are expected to rely on communications, financial evidence, travel records, surveillance, videos, witness testimony and other evidence to try to connect Durk to the alleged plot.

The Defense Says the Government Has It Wrong

Durk's attorneys presented a dramatically different story to the jury.

Defense attorney Marissa Goldberg told jurors that Durk had nothing to do with the murder plot.

Instead, the defense has pointed toward Kavon Grant, a former personal assistant to Durk who has pleaded guilty and is cooperating with federal prosecutors.

THE DEFENSE'S CASE

Durk's defense argues that Grant was responsible for important logistics surrounding the events, including hotels, vehicles and the purchase of ski masks.

The defense contends that after becoming a cooperating witness, Grant had an incentive to minimize his own responsibility and provide prosecutors with someone more prominent.

The defense also disputes the government's murder-for-hire theory by questioning where the alleged payment is.

Their position is straightforward: Durk did not order the murder, did not participate in the shooting and should be found not guilty.

The Cooperating Witnesses Could Be Crucial

One of the biggest battles in this trial may involve witnesses who were themselves connected to the alleged conspiracy.

Grant has pleaded guilty and agreed to cooperate with the government. Other alleged co-conspirators have also entered plea agreements and are expected to testify.

That creates an important question for the jury.

Are cooperating witnesses providing the inside story of what actually happened? Or are they shifting responsibility in hopes of helping themselves?

The jury will have to evaluate their testimony alongside the government's physical, digital and documentary evidence.

Rap Music Is Entering the Courtroom Too

The case has also revived a controversial question: When should an artist's music be used as evidence in a criminal trial?

The judge has allowed prosecutors to introduce certain Lil Durk songs and videos, including material the government argues helps establish motive.

The defense argues that Durk's lyrics are art and performance rather than literal admissions of criminal conduct.

That distinction could become one of the most closely watched parts of the case.

What Is Proven — and What Is Alleged?

DOCUMENTED

Saviay'a Robinson was killed in the August 2022 Los Angeles shooting.

Quando Rondo was present and survived.

Lil Durk has been federally charged.

He has pleaded not guilty.

Several other defendants have entered plea agreements.

A jury has been selected and the trial is now underway.

THE GOVERNMENT MUST PROVE

That Durk ordered or knowingly participated in the alleged murder-for-hire conspiracy.

That money or resources attributed to him were knowingly provided to further the plot.

That communications prosecutors attribute to the conspiracy mean what the government says they mean.

That cooperating witnesses are credible where their testimony implicates Durk.

Why This Trial Matters Beyond Lil Durk

There is obviously a massive amount at stake for Durk personally. A conviction on the most serious charges could mean life in federal prison.

But the case also touches several larger issues that have followed hip-hop into courtrooms for decades.

How should rap lyrics be interpreted? How much weight should juries give cooperating witnesses? Where does an artist's public persona end and evidence of actual intent begin? And most importantly: Can prosecutors connect the person accused of ordering a crime to the people who allegedly carried it out?

THE BLIND TRUTH TAKE

This is exactly the kind of case where people can decide what they believe before the evidence ever reaches the jury. Durk's fans may believe he's innocent. People who read the indictment may already believe he's guilty. Neither one is the legal standard. A headline isn't a conviction. A rap lyric isn't automatically a confession. And a cooperating witness isn't automatically telling the truth or lying simply because he took a deal. The question is whether prosecutors can connect Durk to this alleged murder-for-hire plot with evidence strong enough to prove the charges beyond a reasonable doubt. That's what this trial is supposed to determine.
